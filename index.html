About Me

Curiosity, then a career.

I got into technology the way most people in this field did: by taking things apart to see how they worked, then learning how to put them back together better. That curiosity turned into a ten-year career in enterprise IT.

I started on the sales engineering side at Ricoh, designing and demonstrating enterprise software solutions for large clients. Those years taught me discovery, requirements gathering, and how to explain complex systems to people who don't live in them. Then I moved into hands-on systems engineering, where I now design and support high-availability infrastructure: virtualization, SQL Server availability groups, identity, integrations, and the monitoring that keeps it all honest.

These days my focus is infrastructure, automation, and the practical side of AI. My home lab is where I test ideas, build tools I actually use, and pick up skills my day job doesn't hand me. I've started documenting everything as I go, because a project I can't explain is a project I only half understand.

Outside of work: two kids, a home lab that is always one project deeper than planned, and an unreasonable amount of emotional investment in Mizzou and the Cardinals.
